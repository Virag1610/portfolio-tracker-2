Money in / out & XIRR

Both XIRR figures use only the dates and amounts of your own entries, plus today's value at the latest official closing price. No daily price history is needed. Each table ends with a check row: at the XIRR rate, the discounted amounts add up to zero.

XIRR: your stock trades

Each buy is money paid on its date (including slippage and charges). Each sale and dividend is money received. The last line is the market value of the shares you still hold (shares x latest close), dated today.

Fund XIRR: money added and withdrawn

Each "add money" is paid in on its date, each withdrawal comes back. The last line is today's total value: shares at the latest close plus idle cash. Idle cash pulls this figure down, as it would in real life.

Settings & data

One-time connection, cost assumptions and backups.

1. Connect to your GitHub storage

Your entries are saved into your GitHub repository so the daily price job knows which stocks to fetch, and so your data is safe and visible on any device. The key stays only in this browser. See the README for the 2-minute steps to create it.

2. Trading costs (delivery trades)

Defaults are the official statutory rates for equity delivery: STT 0.1% on buy and sell; stamp duty 0.015% on buy; NSE transaction charge 0.00297% and BSE 0.00375% (group A/B, effective 1 Oct 2024); SEBI fee ₹10 per crore; GST 18% on brokerage + transaction + SEBI charges; depository charge ₹13 + GST per stock per sell day (Zerodha rate). Brokerage is 0 by default (discount brokers charge zero on delivery). Change any value to match your broker.

3. Slippage assumption by company size

Slippage is the gap between the price you see and the price you actually get. There is no official standard for it, so these are starting assumptions: smaller, less traded companies get a bigger gap. It is charged on both buying and selling. Size groups follow NSE's ranking by full market value (Nifty Microcap 250 = ranks 501 to 750).
